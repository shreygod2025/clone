Kits, Components & Originality
Q11. Is the official OLL kit compulsory?
Answer: No. The kit is recommended, but students can purchase their own components.
Q12. Can we build our own bot instead of using the official kit?
Answer: Yes. Bots must follow the specifications and be of the team's original design.
Q13. Can we use motors, sensors, wheels or other components from existing kits?
Answer: Yes, you can use individual hardware components like DC motors, LEGO motors,
wheels, sensors, or chassis plates from existing kits. However, the overall vehicle cannot be
a pre-assembled, ready-made commercial toy car or robot. The assembly, mounting, wiring,
and integration must be done entirely by the students.

Q14. Can we use 3D-printed parts in our bot?
Answer: Yes. Custom 3D-printed brackets, mounts, spacers, or chassis frames are
completely allowed and encouraged, as long as the complete bot stays within the maximum
size and weight limits for your track.
Q15. Can we use Arduino, ESP32 or other microcontrollers?
Answer: Yes. You are completely free to use microcontrollers such as Arduino, ESP32,
Raspberry Pi Pico, or micro:bit for controlling your motors, managing wireless
communication, or running autonomous routines
.
Q16. Can we use ChatGPT-generated code or ready-made Arduino code if we understand
how it works?
Answer: You can use open-source libraries or sample code snippets as reference points to
learn, but the overall logic and final program must be adapted, configured, and understood
by the team. During the technical inspection and judging Q&A, students must be able to
explain how their code and circuits function. Direct copying of ready-made projects without
original contribution can lead to disqualification.
Q17. Can we use a ready-made or pre-programmed bot?
Answer: No. Ready-made bots are not permitted. The bot must be the team's own original
design.
Q18. What are the rules regarding originality of control logic and code?
Answer:The chassis, drivetrain, and control logic must be the team's own original design and
assembly. Any submission that uses a pre-built commercial solution or copies existing work
without meaningful, original student modifications will be disqualified.
Q19. When will we receive the official kit after ordering?
Answer: Within two weeks of ordering.
RoboSumo FAQs
Q20. What is RoboSumo?
Answer: RoboSumo is a head-to-head competition in which two bots compete inside a
circular arena. The objective is to push the opponent out of the arena.
Q21. Can RoboSumo bots be autonomous, wired or wireless?
Answer: Yes. Fully autonomous, wired remote-controlled and wireless remote-controlled bots
are permitted.
Q22. What are the RoboSumo bot size and weight limits?
Answer: Maximum size: 25 × 25 × 25 cm. Maximum weight: 3 kg, including the battery.
Q23. What battery voltage is allowed for RoboSumo?
Answer: The nominal battery voltage must not exceed 12V.
Q24. Can we use six motors in RoboSumo?

Answer: Yes, provided the bot follows the size, weight, voltage and safety requirements.
Q25. What parts or mechanisms are prohibited in RoboSumo?
Answer: Weapons, sharp or pinching mechanisms, flipping mechanisms, projectiles and
mechanisms designed to damage the opponent are prohibited.
Q26. What happens if a RoboSumo match ends without either bot being pushed out?
Answer: The referee/judges decide the winner based on control and dominance
demonstrated during the match. The referee's decision is final.
Q27. What happens if a RoboSumo bot becomes non-functional during a match?
Answer: If both bots are unable to fight or move, the referee grants a 2-minute grace period
for teams to fix their bots. If a bot cannot be made functional within this window, it will lead to
disqualification. Participants cannot touch or manually interfere with their bot during a match
without explicit referee permission.
RoboRacer FAQs
Q29. What is RoboRacer?
Answer: RoboRacer is a head-to-head racing competition in which two bots race on adjacent
lanes. The first bot to cross the finish line wins.
Q30. Is RoboRacer wireless RC only, or can it be wired?
Answer: Fully wireless remote control is required. Wired control is not permitted.
Q31. What are the RoboRacer bot size and weight limits?
Answer: Maximum size: 20 × 15 × 15 cm. Maximum weight: 1.5 kg, including the battery.
Q32. What are the RoboRacer track dimensions and conditions?
Answer: The track is approximately 10–12 metres long, with two adjacent lanes
approximately 40–50 cm wide each. The lanes are separated by clear markings. Exact
venue dimensions may vary.
Q33. What is the width of one RoboRacer lane?
Answer: Approximately 40–50 cm.
Q34. What battery voltage should be used for RoboRacer?
Answer: You can use any standard battery pack (such as a 7.4V 2S LiPo or a 9V–12V pack),
provided the total voltage stays within standard safety limits (12V nominal recommended)
and the total car weight remains under 1.5 kg including the battery.
Q35. Can we use a microcontroller for wireless control in RoboRacer?
Answer: Yes. You can use an ESP32, Arduino paired with a Bluetooth/radio module, or
Raspberry Pi Pico to manage wireless communication via a custom smartphone app,
Bluetooth gamepad, or transmitter.

Q36. Does the RoboRacer kit include an Arduino/ESP32, sensors and a motor driver?
Answer: No. The standard RoboRacer kit includes an RC chassis, motors, wheels, battery
setup, power switch, and a direct radio-frequency remote control system. It does not come
with an Arduino, ESP32, or autonomous sensors.
Q37. Is the RoboRacer kit pre-programmed, and can it be custom-programmed?
Answer: The official kit operates using direct radio transmitter-receiver hardware, so it is not
a pre-programmed computer. If you want to custom-program throttle curves or steering trim,
you are free to add your own microcontroller board like an ESP32 or Arduino.
Q38. Can we connect our own ESP32 or use our own components instead of the official kit?
Answer: Yes, absolutely. You can use your own ESP32, custom motor driver, steering servo,
and independent chassis parts, provided the completed car is fully wireless, stays under 1.5
kg, and fits inside the 20 × 15 × 15 cm limit.
Q39. What mechanisms are prohibited in RoboRacer?
Answer: Mechanisms designed to obstruct, block or damage the opposing bot, along with
sharp or protruding parts and projectiles, are prohibited.
Q40. What happens if a RoboRacer race ends in a tie?
Answer: A tie or photo finish results in an immediate rerun between the same two bots.

Q44. Should the video and documents be uploaded separately or together?
Answer: Upload them together in one Google Drive link. Ensure the link is set to “Anyone

Q45. What does “working or partially working model” mean?
Answer: It means you must show a physical prototype rather than just a concept sketch or
presentation slide. A working model performs the full intended task, while a partially working
model demonstrates core operations (such as a sensor triggering an alert, a motor
mechanism running, or data logging) even if the secondary casing or final features are still
being refined.

Q46. How much functionality is required for a partially working model?
Answer: The model must clearly demonstrate its main operating principle. For example, if
you are designing a smart waste sorter, the sorting sensor and motor mechanism should
physically activate and move during the video, showing that the core engineering logic
works.
Q47. How is Round 1 evaluated?

Problem relevance and clarity: 20%

Design clarity: 20%
Working/partial model demonstration: 30%
Q48. What happens in Round 2 at the Grand Finale?

participate in a 5-minute Q&A with the judges.
Q49. How is Round 2 evaluated?
Answer:
Originality and creativity: 30%
Technical execution: 35%
Real-world relevance: 15%

Section 1: Grade, Eligibility, and Team Composition
Can a seventh grader participate in the senior category? No. The junior category is
strictly for grades 4 to 7, while the senior category is reserved for grades 8 to 12. A
seventh-grade student must compete in the junior block.
Is there any exception to allow students below Grade 4 to participate? Normally,
students below grade 4 are not eligible.
Can we mix students of different grades on the same team? Yes. You can merge
different grades on a team as long as all members belong to the same age block. The
primary/junior block spans grades 4 to 7, and the secondary/senior block spans grades 8
to 12.
Can a student participate in multiple tracks or join different teams if the school has very
few students? No. Each student is strictly restricted to one team and one track because
multiple track events (such as Robo Racer and Robo Innovation) will run simultaneously
during the Grand Finale. Organizers do not allow students to be duplicated across
competitions. If a school has exactly five students, they can be split into separate teams
(e.g., three students on a Robo Sumo team and two students on an Innovation Challenge
team).
Section 2: Logistics, Chaperones, and Certificates
If school teachers are unavailable to travel in December, can a parent chaperone the
teams? Yes. One adult guardian—either a teacher or a parent per school—is permitted to
accompany all of a school's qualifying teams.
Will a parent chaperone receive an official certificate? Certificates are printed based on
registration data. If the parent registers on the portal officially as the team's mentor or
coordinator, they will receive a printed certificate from IIT Bombay Techfest. If a teacher
registers but cannot travel, only the registered teacher's certificate is printed.
How does an educator link multiple student teams to their mentor registration? After
registering on the mentor panel, the educator must enter the unique Team IDs or student
Competition IDs for all the teams they are supervising to map them together on the
portal.
Section 3: Technical Rules & Arena Doubts (Robo Sumo)
If a tiny piece of our robot breaks off and falls outside the ring while the main body is
inside, are we out? Yes. A robot is declared out the exact moment any part of it (even a
single wheel or a small broken-off piece) crosses the boundary line of the 100 cm circular
ring.

Can we use an extending ramp or pushing arm in Robo Sumo? Yes, pushing
mechanisms are allowed. However, the robot must still fit within the strict 25 cm x 25 cm
x 25 cm dimensional envelope even when its mechanisms are fully extended or in
motion.
Is physical attacking or flipping allowed? No. Attacking, flipping, or any mechanism
designed to damage or block the opposing robot is strictly prohibited. Rotating parts are
only allowed if they do not harm the opponent.
What happens if a robot breaks down during an active match? If a bot breaks down, the
team is given a strict 2-minute grace period to repair it. Students cannot touch the robot
during an active match unless they receive explicit approval from the referee. If it is not
running after 2 minutes, the team is disqualified, and the functional bot wins. If both
break down simultaneously and cannot be repaired in 2 minutes, both are disqualified.
Section 4: Technical Rules & Arena Doubts (Robo Racer)
Can students use mobile apps to control the Robo Racer? Yes. Mobile phones are
permitted to control the racer wirelessly via Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, provided they do not
cause signal interference with the opponent.
What happens if a racer accidentally drives into the adjacent lane? Entering or
obstructing the opponent's lane is strictly prohibited. The referee will either disqualify the
offending bot or call for an immediate re-race depending on the severity and intent of the
interference.
Are there any curves, loops, or elevations on the Robo Racer track? No. The Robo Racer
track is a completely straight path roughly 10 to 12 meters long with no curves or
elevations. There may be minor straight obstacles to jump over.
Section 5: Robotics Innovation Challenge Clarifications
Is a microcontroller like Arduino mandatory for the Innovation Challenge? No. There are
no hardware or software restrictions in the Innovation Challenge. Junior teams (grades
4–7) are not required to use microcontrollers and can build simple circuits or machines.
While microcontrollers like Arduino or basic IoT logic are preferred for senior teams
(grades 8–12) to demonstrate higher technical complexity, they are not strictly forced.
Can we build a daily life solution project that does not use robotics? While daily life
solutions are allowed, it is highly recommended to build a project with robotics
components. Projects using robotics will be awarded higher marks because the
evaluation rubrics place significant weight on technical functionality.
Section 6: Electronics, Kit Sourcing, and Budgets
Why are standard Vidblocks/Witblocks electronics disqualified from Robo Sumo and
Robo Racer? Standard electronics from Vidblocks/Witblocks are classified as
"readymade kits," which are banned in Robo Sumo and Robo Racer. Students must
assemble the electronics themselves using open-source components. However, students
are permitted to use physical building blocks or acrylic bodies from their school kits to
build their robot's external chassis.
Are we required to buy the kits sold on the OLL website? No, purchasing the kits on the
website is completely optional. They consist of standard open-source components

compiled in one place for convenience. You can buy equivalent parts from any local
electronics market.
What is the estimated budget for materials if students build their own robot? The cost of
components is estimated between 1,000 and 3,500/4,000 rupees depending on the kit
selected. Since participation is team-based, this cost can be divided among the 3 to 5
team members.
Section 7: Guaranteed Entry Policies for Partner/Customer Schools
What are the exact guaranteed entry rules for customer/partner schools? All OLL
customer schools receive guaranteed selection for the Grand Finale of the Robotics
Innovation Challenge (one primary/junior team and one secondary/senior team). They

If a customer school registers multiple teams, do all of them get guaranteed entries?
No. The guarantee applies to only one team per age category per competition. The
school teacher must designate which team gets the guaranteed spot. All other teams
from that school must go through the standard online selection process.

Section 2: Track 1: Robo Sumo Rules & Specifications
● What is the core objective of Robo Sumo? The goal is to push the opponent's
robot entirely out of the arena. The match is a pure test of engineering, control, and
pushing strategy.
● What are the dimensional and weight specifications for a Sumo Bot?
○ Dimensions: The bot must fit entirely within a 25 cm x 25 cm x 25 cm
envelope (length, width, and height). This limit is strict and applies even when
the robot is in motion or has mechanisms extended.
○ Weight: The maximum weight limit is 3 kg, which includes the battery. There
is no grace weight limit during the mandatory technical inspection.
● What control mechanisms are allowed? Sumo bots can be fully autonomous,
wired, or controlled via wireless remote or phone.
● What are the battery voltage rules for Robo Sumo? The total nominal battery
voltage cannot exceed 12 volts. Rechargeable batteries are permitted. If a fully
charged 12V battery temporarily reads slightly higher (e.g., 12.3V or 12.4V) at the
start of a match, it is acceptable as long as its nominal rating is 12V.
● What constitutes an elimination or a "knockout"? A robot is considered
eliminated the exact moment any part of the bot (including a single wheel or a small
piece that breaks and falls off) crosses the outer boundary line of the circular ring.
● What are the dimensions of the Robo Sumo ring? The arena is a circular ring with
a diameter of 100 cm and is marked with a clear boundary line.
● What happens if both bots exit the ring simultaneously? The referee will
determine which bot crossed the line first. If the referee cannot determine this, the
match is immediately restarted.
● What are the rules regarding weapon systems or flipping mechanisms? All
weapons, flipping mechanisms, or devices designed to damage, block, or harm the
opposing bot are strictly prohibited. Rotating mechanisms are allowed only if they
are not designed to harm the opponent.
● What happens if a bot breaks down during a match? If a robot breaks down in the
arena, the team is given a strict 2-minute grace period to fix it. If it cannot be

repaired within 2 minutes, the team is disqualified, and the functional bot takes the
win. If both bots break down simultaneously and cannot be fixed within 2 minutes,
both teams are disqualified.
● Are students allowed to touch their robot during a match? No. Manual
interference or touching the robot is strictly forbidden once the match begins,
unless explicitly approved and permitted by the referee.

● What are the age groups and themes?
○ Junior Category (Grades 4–7): Theme is "Smart solutions for everyday
life". Teams must build basic working models using simple circuits or recycled
materials focusing on topics like waste segregation or plant care.
○ Senior Category (Grades 8–12): Theme is "Robotics and automation for
a smart and sustainable future". Teams must demonstrate advanced
functional prototypes using microcontrollers (like Arduino) or basic IoT logic
targeting complex problems like smart agriculture or disaster management.
● What are the mandatory submissions for Round 1 (Online)? Teams must submit
one Google Drive link containing a folder with these five items:
○ Official team registration details.
○ A one-page problem writeup stating the issue they are addressing.
○ A 500-to-800-word technical abstract describing their technology,
components, and originality. This abstract must not be written using AI
tools like ChatGPT or Gemini.
○ A design diagram, sketch, or CAD drawing.
○ A 1-to-3 minute video demonstration showing the working prototype with
the faces of all team members clearly visible.
● Note: Teams can compile these documents into a single PDF if preferred, but all
items must reside in one enabled Drive link.
● What are the evaluation rubrics for both rounds?
○ Round 1 (Online Selection): Problem and relevance (20%), Originality &
Technical soundness of the abstract (30%), Design clarity (20%), Working
model/Partial demonstration (30%).
○ Round 2 (Grand Finale live presentation at IIT Bombay): Originality &
Creativity of the idea (30%), Technical execution/bringing the concept to life
(35%), Real-world relevance (15%), Presentation and Technical Q&A (20%).
● Can teams upgrade their prototypes before the Grand Finale? Yes. Teams are
explicitly permitted to bring an upgraded or improved version of their model to the
Grand Finale.
● Is there a special rule for OOL partner/customer schools? Yes. All OOL customer
schools are guaranteed selection for the Grand Finale of the Innovation
Challenge (one primary team and one secondary team per school). They must still
submit their online materials for review, but their spot in the finals is guaranteed.
Section 5: Technical Components, Kits, and Battery Rules
● Are existing school kits (like Vidblocks/Witblocks) allowed in the Sumo and
Racer tracks? No. Standard readymade electronics kits like Vidblocks/Witblocks are
classified as "readymade kits" and will be disqualified if used as the internal

electronics or control cards in Robo Sumo and Robo Racer. However, teams are
allowed to use the physical building blocks, structural bricks, or acrylic bodies from
these kits for external decoration/chassis support.
● Are Vidblocks allowed in the Robotics Innovation Challenge? Yes. Electronic
parts and modules from Vidblocks/Witblocks are fully permitted for the Innovation
Challenge.
● Are teams required to purchase the kits listed on the website? No. Purchasing
the kits is completely optional. They are compiled open-source components
(motors, chassis, drivers) provided in one place for ease of access so students do
not have to hunt for parts in the market.
● Are microcontrollers like Arduino mandatory for Sumo and Racer? No. Arduino
is optional for Sumo and Racer. These bots can be wired directly to function or use a
basic open-source wireless TX/RX system. Arduino is only necessary if a team
decides to program a Bluetooth mobile control app.
Section 6: Event Logistics, Deadlines, and Zonal Hubs
○ December 31st: Final deadline for winners to submit their bank details form
to claim prize money.
● Where are the five regional/zonal on-ground qualification hubs?
○ Mumbai (West Hub)
○ Kanpur (East Hub)
○ Delhi (North Hub)
○ Bangalore (South Hub)
○ Indore (Central Hub)
● How will participation and winning certificates be distributed?
○ Standard Participation: Issued as a soft copy.
○ Zonal Qualifiers & Finalists: Issued as a hard copy. If a school is easily
accessible by the team, organizers will print and deliver the physical
certificate directly to the school.
● Who is responsible for travel and accommodation expenses? All travel, stay,
and logistical expenses for regional rounds and the Grand Finale must be borne
individually by the participating teams or their respective schools.
