# Techfest, IIT Bombay — Techfest 2026-27 — OLL Robotics Championship

## Objective
The Techfest's OLL Robotics Championship gives school students across the country a structured, credible platform to design, build, and compete with robots, and to pitch original engineering ideas in front of a national panel. Built around three tracks so that students with different strengths — mechanical building, dynamic control, or research and innovation — all have a track suited to them.

Visit **oll.co/techfest** for latest updates and detailed videos.

## The Three Tracks
- **Track 1 – RoboSumo** — Head-to-head pushing contest between two bots inside a circular ring, testing structural design, traction, and pushing power. Rules adapted from the Technoxian World Robotics Championship Sumo Bot format.
- **Track 2 – Roboracer** — Head-to-head speed contest between two RC bots on a shared track, testing drivetrain design, agility, and race-line strategy.
- **Track 3 – Robotics Innovation Challenge** — Research-and-presentation track: students identify a real-world problem and present an original robotic prototype or automation concept. Two age categories: Junior (grades 4–7), Senior (grades 8–12).

## Qualification Pathway (RoboSumo & Roboracer only)
### Round 1 — National Online Qualifier (Mandatory)
Every team must submit:
- Technical Abstract (500–800 words) covering:
  1. Team & bot name, one-line design approach
  2. Mechanical design — chassis, dimensions/weight, key structural choices
  3. Drivetrain & motors — motor type/count, wheels, battery/voltage
  4. Control system — autonomous / wired / wireless (Track 1); RC setup (Track 2)
  5. Match strategy — pushing/defense (Track 1) or driving approach (Track 2)
- Short video demonstration of the bot.
Teams that clear Round 1 qualify for their nearest OLL Regional Qualifier.

## Track 1 — RoboSumo
RoboSumo turns mechanical design and control programming into a head-to-head duel. Fully autonomous or wireless bots push opponents clean over the edge. No weapons, no flipping — just raw traction, weight distribution, and sharp control.

### Arena
- Shape & size: Circular, ~**100 cm diameter**.
- Boundary: Clearly defined circular line. A bot is OUT the moment any part (even one wheel) crosses it.

### Bot Specs
- Size envelope: **25 × 25 × 25 cm max** at all times (including extended mechanisms).
- Max weight: **3 kg including battery**.
- Control: Fully autonomous, wired, or wireless RC — all permitted.
- Prohibited: Weapons, sharp/pinching/flipping mechanisms, projectiles, anything designed to damage the opponent.
- Build origin: Team's own original design and construction; ready-made kits/bots NOT permitted.
- Recommended kit (optional): https://oll.co/shop/product/robo-sumo-kit-specially-for-techfest-iit-bombay-22b5228a

### Battery
- Nominal voltage must NOT exceed **12V**. Rechargeable batteries OK. A fully charged 12V pack reading slightly higher (12.3–12.4V) at match start is acceptable.

### Match Structure
- 1v1 face-off, bots at opposite starting points.
- Match = up to **2 min**; first bot pushed out of the arena wins.
- If neither bot is pushed out → 2 restart / sudden-death rounds; after that, referee decides on control & dominance. Referee's decision is final.
- No manual interference by participants unless referee permits.
- Non-functional bot gets **2 min grace period** to fix; otherwise forfeits.
- Knockout bracket — winner advances, loser eliminated.

### Evaluation
Pure match outcome — no separate scoring unless there's a tie.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
## Track 2 — Roboracer
Head-to-head RC race on a shared track. Standing start to finish line. Lives or dies by traction, steering, driver reflexes.

### Arena & Bot
- Track: ~**5 m long**, **2 parallel lanes** (~40 cm wide each), separated by clear lane lines. Start/finish lines marked. Exact dimensions may vary by venue.
- Bot envelope: **20 × 20 × 15 cm max**.
- Max weight: **1.5 kg including battery**.
- Control: Fully **wireless RC only** — one frequency/channel per team to prevent interference.
- Surface: Non-slip vinyl or mat with painted lane markings.
- Finish line: Bot must FULLY cross to be considered finished.
- Prohibited: Obstruction, blocking, damage mechanisms, sharp/protruding parts, projectiles.
- Build origin: Chassis, drivetrain, control logic must be the team's own. Ready-made RC toy cars NOT permitted.
- Recommended kit (optional): https://oll.co/shop/product/robo-racer-kit-specially-made-for-iit-bombay-8bf545dc

### Race Structure
- 1v1 heats in adjacent lanes, simultaneous start on referee signal.
- Each heat ≤ **2 min**; first bot to FULLY cross the finish line wins.
- Dead heat/photo finish → immediate re-race between the same two bots.
- Knockout bracket.

### Evaluation
Pure race outcome.

## Track 3 — Robotics Innovation Challenge
Not a head-to-head track. Students spot a real-world problem, design an original robotics/automation/STEM prototype, and pitch it. Two-round structure — no Regional bypass applies.

### Age Categories & Themes
- **Junior (Grades 4–7)** — Theme: *Smart Solutions for Everyday Life*. Simple problems from school/home/neighbourhood (waste segregation, water conservation, road/school safety, plant care, home help, energy saving). Basic circuits + recycled materials expected — working physical model matters more than complexity.
- **Senior (Grades 8–12)** — Theme: *Robotics & Automation for a Smart, Sustainable Future*. Technically demanding problems (smart agriculture, disaster management, healthcare & assistive tech, sustainable energy, smart mobility, environmental monitoring). May use microcontrollers (Arduino, Raspberry Pi, micro:bit) and sensors; working principle required, not just concept sketch.

### Round 1 — Online Qualifier (deadline 20 Oct 2026)
Submit a SINGLE Google Drive link (viewer access enabled) containing:
1. **Team registration details** — team name, school/RoboClub name, category, members + mentor.
2. **Problem write-up (1 page)** — the real-world problem, why it matters, who it affects.
3. **Technical abstract (500–800 words)** — proposed solution, how it works, components, originality. **Must NOT be AI-written.**
4. **Design diagram / sketch / CAD** — labelled.
5. **Video demonstration (1–3 min)** — working or partially-working model in action. **All team members' faces must be clearly visible.**

### Round 1 Evaluation
| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Problem relevance & clarity (write-up) | 20% |
| Originality & technical soundness of the abstract | 30% |
| Design clarity (diagram/sketch) | 20% |
| Working / partial model demonstrated (video) | 30% |

### Round 2 — Grand Finale (IIT Bombay)
- Prototype display at an assigned space (upgraded Round-1 model allowed).
- **10-minute live demo** showing problem-to-solution flow.
- **5-minute Q&A** with judges on technical working, design choices, feasibility.
- Junior: conversational and encouraging. Senior: more detailed technical walkthrough.

### Round 2 Evaluation
| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Originality & creativity of the idea | 30% |
| Technical execution of the prototype | 35% |
| Real-world relevance | 15% |
| Presentation & Q&A | 20% |

Round 1 is used only to shortlist; final rankings are based on Round 2.

## COMPONENT / BUILD FAQs (quick answers)
- **Can we use 3D-printed parts?** Yes — brackets, mounts, spacers, chassis frames. Must stay within size/weight limits.
- **Can we use Arduino/ESP32/Raspberry Pi/micro:bit?** Yes, freely.
- **Can we use ChatGPT-generated or ready-made Arduino code?** As a reference only. Final logic must be adapted and understood by the team. Direct copying → disqualification.
- **Can we reuse motors/sensors/wheels/chassis plates from existing kits?** YES for individual components. NO for pre-assembled commercial toy cars/robots. All assembly, mounting, wiring, integration must be done by the students.
- **Are Blix / Witblox / Avishkaar / Vidblocks electronics allowed?** NO in RoboSumo and Roboracer (classified as "ready-made"). YES for Robotics Innovation Challenge. Structural bricks / acrylic bodies from these kits ARE allowed for external chassis in all tracks.
- **Is the official OLL kit compulsory?** No — strongly recommended but optional. Equivalent parts from any electronics market OK.
- **Kit delivery time:** Within 2 weeks of ordering.
- **RoboSumo — can we use 6 motors?** Yes, as long as size/weight/voltage/safety limits are met.
- **RoboRacer — mobile-phone control?** Yes, over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi, no frequency interference with opponent.
- **RoboSumo — pushing arm / extending ramp?** Allowed. Robot must still fit the 25×25×25 cm envelope even when extended.

## REGISTRATION PATHWAYS
Three pathways:
1. **Individual Student Registration** — student registers themselves, picks a track.
2. **Educator / Mentor Registration** — teacher/mentor registers to oversee teams.
3. **School Registration** — school admin or RM registers the institution.

### Team registration flow
- Team leader registers first → gets a Competition ID → creates a team → gets a Team ID.
- Leader inputs the exact email addresses of other members.
- Each member then visits the registration link and signs in via Google using THAT exact email → auto-connects to the team.
- Mentor registration: after signing up, the educator enters each team's Team ID (or Competition ID) to map the teams.

Registration fees: **ZERO**. Free participation across all tracks and rounds.
